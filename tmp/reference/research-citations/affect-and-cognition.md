# Affect And Cognition Citations

Research context for affect, cognitive architectures, active inference,
cybernetics, and supervision metaphors. Treat affect and cognition terms as
advisory design language unless a local policy boundary enforces them.

## Selected References

| Reference | Contribution | Local relevance |
|---|---|---|
| Mehrabian (1996); Russell and Mehrabian (1977) | Pleasure/arousal/dominance affect model. | Captured PAD terminology and affect-state sketches. |
| Damasio (1994); Bechara et al. (2000) | Somatic markers and decision bias. | Advisory memory/routing signal, not a safety authority. |
| Ortony, Clore, and Collins (1988); Scherer (2001) | Appraisal theories of emotion. | Background for structured affect events. |
| Gebhard (2005), ALMA | Layered affect over time. | Captured emotion/mood/temperament vocabulary. |
| Kahneman (2011) | Fast/slow cognition. | Useful shorthand for routing tiers; avoid treating it as a literal implementation model. |
| Sumers et al. (2023), CoALA | Cognitive architecture for language agents. | Agent loop and memory/action categories. |
| Friston (2006); Parr, Pezzulo, and Friston (2022) | Free energy and active inference. | Scoring inspiration for context selection and routing. |
| Woolley et al. (2010) | Collective intelligence factor. | Use as a measurement analogy for multi-agent work. |
| Wiener (1948), Ashby (1956), Beer (1972) | Cybernetics, requisite variety, viable systems. | Background for supervision and conductor-style monitors. |
| Yerkes and Dodson (1908) | Pressure/arousal and performance curve. | Cautionary analogy for intervention aggressiveness. |

## Use In IronClaw

- Keep affect-derived signals advisory until explicit policy says otherwise.
- Do not let affect, reputation, or learned confidence bypass approvals,
  sandboxing, or auth.
- Prefer measurable state and event contracts over anthropomorphic wording.
